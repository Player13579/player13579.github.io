## Declared phenomenon and visual causality

PH1: declared fantasy energy supply creates a ground-attached hot participating-gas field. The actual fuel chemistry is unknown; fuel is a normalized visual supply control, not a claimed real chemical simulation. Spatial closure: elliptic ground footprint, y>0, finite rising volume and finite time. It neither fires a bullet nor hits an invented receiver. Main shape is a broad irregular root feeding tapering flame tongues with rolling internal folds and translucent gaps. Macro is the connected root/plume; meso is advected reaction folds and narrowing tip; micro is a limited second noise octave for gas interface variation. No decal, rune, orb, rings, extraneous sparks or debris are used. Flame is read through rising shape and supply dependence rather than color or a label.

Coordinates: x right, y up (opposite gravity), z view depth. Orthographic pitched projection screenY=y−.36z. 24 front-to-back depth samples integrate participating-gas extinction and radiance. Buoyant advection is a kinematic approximation: upward phase velocity and height-dependent lateral entrainment, with retarded ground supply at height. This is not a Navier–Stokes solver or exact blackbody flame simulation. Separate density, reaction/temperature proxy and emission are used; density is not simply the emission value. Beer–Lambert transmittance composes the finite volume over the scene once. No opaque smoke is invented to conceal the flame.

PH2: immediate reflected light on the existing synthetic floor/person fixture. A three-height normalized emitter approximation drives local received orange light with distance falloff and surface-mask weighting. It is an approximate fixture response, not full global illumination or verified real character material registration. Source relocation moves the field/light while the reference silhouette stays fixed. There is no invented mechanical response, scorch or damage. Source-off removes both PH1 and PH2.

Time L: [0,.10L) ignition front spreads over finite supply footprint and gas rises; [.10L,.78L) sustained connected root and advected flame folds; [.78L,L) supply ends at root, last supplied gas continues upward, lower flame extinguishes before upper remainder; L and after clear the source, light and all source-bound optical contribution exactly. The latest height supply ends before L (maximum .99825L), so finite closure does not rely on a long unconnected afterglow. There is no periodic light pulse. Reduced motion slows only the spatial advection factor, preserving supply timeline, emission and expiry.

## Observation and intensity

OBS1 heat refraction: screen-space approximation of view through hot gas. Input is the integrated PH1 heat column; central differences yield a source-local displacement of scene radiance. It does not move the flame geometry or person input. This is not a complete physical refractive-index solver. OBS2 local glare: emission-only finite PSF neighborhood (eight samples, radius .115 H) adds source-bound radiance. It represents a simple display/eye spread; no camera lens or aperture ghost is claimed. Flare/ghost evaluated: no lens model specified, extended flame occupies many pixels, so isolated geometric ghosts and star rays are not selected. They would compete with the advected hot-gas main shape. Thermal refraction and local glare are selected as contextual optical responses rather than mandatory generic decoration.

OBS3 display mapping: linear HDR radiance -> exponential exposure response -> one linear-to-sRGB encode into non-sRGB preferred canvas format. HDR scene / PH emission / medium have separate rgba16float targets. No background-dependent gain and no white-clipping defect claim. Intensity constants are R1 authored choices pending actual H64 assessment, not universal limits.
